Research

My research focuses on representing and learning physical systems efficiently. I combine neural operators, mathematical modeling, large-scale simulations, and measured signals, with a primary emphasis on wireless propagation and sensing.

Physics-unrolled neural operators for radio maps (RayNO)

High-fidelity site-specific ray tracing is expensive, and generating clean training labels is itself a computational bottleneck. RayNO predicts a high-fidelity radio map from a low-fidelity ray-tracing input plus scene geometry. Its three stages correspond to specular reflection, diffraction and scattering. Rather than forcing one network to learn all effects, the formulation gradually adds physical complexity.

Our analysis shows that under conditionally unbiased simulation label errors, the learned operator can outperform the labels it was trained on. We validate this assumption using repeated simulation seeds and test generalization to unseen indoor layouts and noise conditions. The system achieves approximately 250× faster prediction compared with high-fidelity simulation in the reported setting.

Physics-based channel compression (GCNO)

Deep-learning CSI compressors usually rely on a fixed-size code tied to a particular antenna configuration. GCNO instead estimates the directions and complex gains of dominant propagation paths. Its Gramian Chebyshev operator adapts filtering to the observed channel while keeping learned coefficients shared across antenna sizes; an analytic decoder reconstructs the channel from the path tuples.

In ray-traced city environments, this approach improves the reconstruction–feedback trade-off against learned baselines and generalizes to antenna-array sizes absent from training. This research grew out of my 2026 internship at InterDigital AI Lab.

Measurement-constrained wireless neural fields (WiNeRF)

WiNeRF learns a spatially continuous, complex-valued wireless channel representation directly from sparse Wi-Fi CSI measured using commodity devices. A cone-based sampling model accounts for the angular resolution of practical antenna arrays, while a multiresolution representation and phase-aware learning objective capture meaningful propagation structure.

The model supports conventional beamforming, angle-of-arrival profiling, and radio coverage mapping without learning a separate task-specific predictor. This work was published at EWSN 2026.

Neural canopy scattering for ray tracing (FoliaRT)

Vegetation propagation is often simplified as attenuation in conventional ray tracers, which neglects direction-dependent scattering. FoliaRT is a collaborative effort to learn reusable scattering responses from electromagnetic simulations, using canopy morphology and incidence conditions to predict transmission, absorption and angular scattering. The surrogate can be integrated into site-specific ray tracing without detailed reconstruction of every leaf or branch.

Evaluations in the submitted manuscript include electromagnetic-model comparisons and measured wireless datasets. This is ongoing work submitted to ACM SenSys 2027; it is not yet a peer-reviewed publication.

Seismic early warning with graph learning

Before joining UIUC, I worked on predicting earthquake shaking from short portions of early seismic waveforms. Our self-supervised contrastive GNN combines information across stations in a graph representing their spatial relationships and uses longer training windows to support prediction from short initial observations. This work appeared in IEEE Transactions on Geoscience and Remote Sensing (2024).
