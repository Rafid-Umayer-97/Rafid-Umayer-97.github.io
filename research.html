Research

I investigate physics-informed machine learning for wireless communication, blending electromagnetic theory with data-driven models to create robust 6G systems.

Research overview

My doctoral work at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign centers on physics-guided AI for wireless propagation, ultra-massive MIMO beamforming, and reconfigurable metasurfaces. By infusing Maxwell's equations, scattering theory, and array processing insights into neural representations, I aim to build reliable channel models and beamformers that generalize across environments and frequency bands.

Prior to UIUC, I completed an M.Sc. (Thesis) in Electrical Engineering at The University of Texas at Dallas, where I focused on RF learning, and a B.Sc. in Electrical & Electronic Engineering at BUET. Those experiences grounded my research in both electromagnetics and applied machine learning.

Current interests

  • Physics-guided deep learning for RF propagation and ultra-massive MIMO beamforming.
  • Neural representations and generative models for wireless channel emulation.
  • Reliable sensing-communication co-design, RIS/metasurface control, and 6G digital twins.

Research highlights

  • MetaFAP Meta-learning for frequency-agnostic prediction of metasurface properties to accelerate reconfigurable intelligent surface control.
  • Singular Vector Projection Novel beamforming strategies for MU-MIMO that ensure interference control while remaining lightweight for deployment.
  • Self-supervised UM-MIMO Contrastive learning techniques that enhance robustness of THz ultra-massive MIMO links under imperfect channel state information.
