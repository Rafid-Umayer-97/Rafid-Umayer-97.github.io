Research agenda

Research

I build machine learning systems that understand the physical structure of wireless environments, from neural RF fields and hybrid neural operators to practical beamforming, sensing, and reconfigurable surfaces.

Physics-informed AI for actionable wireless digital twins

My current PhD research at UIUC explores generative models, NeRF-style representations, and specialized neural operators for object-centric wireless channel modeling with applications in integrated sensing and communications.

Rather than treating wireless channels as black-box matrices, I focus on models that encode multipath structure, antenna observability, hardware limitations, and spatial continuity. The goal is to make learned channels useful for decisions: beam selection, AoA estimation, coverage mapping, simulation, and autonomous network control.

Research themes

Core directions

Neural RF fields

Hardware-constrained neural radiance fields that reconstruct complex-valued CSI from sparse measurements while preserving phase relationships needed for downstream wireless tasks.

Hybrid neural operators

Operator-learning models for wireless field prediction that combine object-centric scene structure with physics-aware inductive biases for spatial generalization.

AI-native 6G systems

Unsupervised CSI compression, spatiotemporal channel forecasting, RIS/metasurface intelligence, and beamforming methods that can run within practical network constraints.

Research highlights

Accepted to EWSN 2026CSI reconstruction

WiNeRF

WiNeRF learns a site-specific channel field from sparse commodity WiFi CSI. Its cone sampling wave model accounts for finite antenna-array angular resolution, outperforming neural baselines by 3–6.7 dB SNR in the paper's evaluation.

Efficient learning for MIMO and metasurfaces

Across IEEE Access, IEEE Communications Letters, ICC Workshops, CISS, EuMC, and MetaFAP, I developed learning and optimization methods for hybrid beamforming, robust THz communications, and frequency-agnostic metasurface prediction.

Accepted to ArrayGPS-denied

I-NAV

I-NAV addresses geolocation when GPS is unavailable by reasoning over questionnaire-based travel cues and feasible routing regions, achieving strong overlap with commercial routing-derived regions.

IEEE TGRSSafety-critical AI

Seismic early warning

I designed a self-supervised contrastive graph neural network for real-time seismic intensity prediction, translating physical wave dynamics into actionable earthquake early warning estimates.
