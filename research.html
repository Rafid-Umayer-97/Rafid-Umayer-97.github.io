Research vision

Scientific AI grounded in physical systems.

My research develops neural operators, measurement-aware learning systems, and physics-based surrogates that reduce simulation cost without discarding the mechanisms that make physical predictions useful. The methods transfer from wireless channels to broader problems involving noisy measurements and expensive simulations.

Flagship · NeurIPS 2026

RayNO: learning high-fidelity fields from cheap simulations.

RayNO (previously developed as PU-HNO) is a three-stage physics-unrolled neural operator. It refines a low-cost radio field through reflection, diffraction and scattering and learns from intermediate-fidelity simulations rather than demanding clean, fully converged labels.

We establish a denoising guarantee under conditionally unbiased label error, test that assumption using independent simulation seeds, and evaluate spatial fidelity, generalization and wireless deployment metrics.

~250× faster high-fidelity prediction

Neural operators · AAAI 2027 under review

GCNO: compact representations with physical meaning.

At InterDigital AI Lab, I developed the Gramian Chebyshev Neural Operator to encode MIMO channels as a variable number of dominant propagation paths. The receiver transmits a short set of physical parameters; the base station reconstructs the channel analytically rather than relying on a learned decoder.

The method matches strong learned baselines at substantially lower feedback and handles unseen antenna array sizes without retraining.

~⅓ feedback bits at comparable reconstruction accuracy

Physics-based simulation · Submitted to SenSys 2027

FoliaRT: reusable neural electromagnetic canopy models.

FoliaRT brings direction-dependent vegetation scattering into site-specific wireless ray tracing. A neural surrogate predicts transmission, absorption, and angular scattering from compact canopy and incident-wave properties, without tracing each leaf or branch in a deployment scene.

Evaluations include electromagnetic simulation, held-out canopy states, and real-world measurements.

53% reduction in median power-prediction error in a field study

Measured wireless data · EWSN 2026

WiNeRF: neural fields from sparse commodity Wi-Fi measurements.

WiNeRF learns continuous, complex-valued channel representations from commodity Wi-Fi CSI. Its model respects antenna geometry, phase uncertainty, and measurement sparsity, supporting reusable predictions for beamforming, AoA estimation and radio coverage.

It combines a differentiable model with actual hardware observations rather than requiring dense external 3D scans.

4.9 dB higher average prediction SNR than neural baselines
