Research

My research spans wireless communications, machine learning, physics-informed modeling, signal processing, and practical sensing systems. I have worked on neural representations of electromagnetic propagation, 6G MIMO and THz communication, intelligent metasurfaces, radar-based sensing, and seismic early warning.

Research directions

  • Physics-based and scientific machine learning: neural operators, differentiable simulation, multi-fidelity learning, and ray-tracing acceleration.
  • Wireless propagation and digital twins: geometry-aware radiance fields, measurement-constrained channel modeling, site-specific RF propagation, and vegetation scattering.
  • Wireless systems and signal processing: MIMO/THz beamforming, channel state information (CSI) compression and tracking, GPU acceleration, and network resource allocation.
  • Intelligent electromagnetic materials: reconfigurable intelligent surfaces, full-wave-model surrogates, and frequency-agnostic meta-learning.
  • Sensing and applied ML: seismic intensity prediction, millimeter-wave radar vital signs, GPS-denied navigation, weather-sensitive wireless networks, and embedded computer vision.

Neural operators for high-fidelity ray tracing (RayNO)

High-fidelity wireless ray tracing is too costly to use for every environment and transmitter configuration. RayNO learns a mapping from low-cost, imperfect simulation outputs to high-fidelity radio maps using a physics-unrolled neural operator. It separates reflection, diffraction, and scattering into supervised stages rather than learning all propagation effects with one black box.

The paper gives a denoising result under conditionally unbiased simulation noise, tests the model across new scenes and conditions, and reports around 250× faster high-fidelity inference than the reference ray-tracing configuration. This research was accepted to NeurIPS 2026.

Physics-based CSI compression and tracking (GCNO)

Traditional CSI feedback requires users to communicate high-dimensional channel matrices. During my 2026 internship at InterDigital AI Lab, I worked on Gramian Chebyshev Neural Operator (GCNO), which infers dominant multipath directions and complex gains, transmits a variable-length physical representation, and reconstructs the channel analytically. In our benchmark, it matched the best of eight learned baselines with roughly one-third the feedback bits and transferred to previously unseen antenna array sizes without retraining.

My related research develops a temporal feedback protocol in which users and base stations maintain a shared path representation and communicate only changes in a mobile channel. On measured data, the path representation required around 400 times fewer values than full CSI. I am also investigating path-parameter-based coordinated beamforming and GPU acceleration on NVIDIA L4, A100, and RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell accelerators.

Neural wireless radiance fields (WiNeRF)

WiNeRF is a measurement-constrained neural field for actionable RF modeling. From sparse commodity Wi-Fi channel-state information, it learns a continuous complex-valued spatial representation while accounting for antenna-array resolution, phase uncertainty, and the constraints of real measurements. This learned field is reusable for coverage prediction, beamforming, and angle-of-arrival profiling rather than being trained for just one downstream task.

Electromagnetic canopy scattering for outdoor wireless simulation (FoliaRT)

Conventional ray tracers often approximate vegetation with scalar attenuation, overlooking directional redistribution of radio energy. FoliaRT learns a reusable electromagnetic response of an entire canopy, conditioned on its morphology and incident wave. The model predicts transmission, absorption, and directional scattering and is designed for integration with existing ray-tracing engines without reconstructing individual leaves or branches.

Our submitted SenSys 2027 manuscript evaluates its predictions against electromagnetic models, unseen canopy configurations, and field measurements. It reports up to 35,000× faster canopy-response computation than the reference electromagnetic scattering model; in an AERPAW study, median RSS prediction error falls from 4.2 dB to 2.0 dB relative to a conventional vegetation baseline. This work is under review at ACM SenSys 2027.

MU-MIMO and THz communications

At BUET and UT Dallas, I developed learning-based and algorithmic approaches to low-latency beamforming, multiuser beam selection, and interference suppression for millimeter-wave and terahertz networks. This includes CNN–LSTM hybrid beamforming, self-supervised contrastive learning under imperfect CSI, and greedy and singular-vector-projection methods that reduce the cost of beam selection.

Reconfigurable intelligent surfaces and metasurface learning

My metasurface research includes frequency-agnostic meta-learning for predicting material response (MetaFAP) and an impedance-regulated deep neural network for RIS-assisted interference suppression (IR-DNN). The wider aim is to replace expensive simulation or iterative optimization with fast learning-based surrogates while preserving relevant electromagnetic behavior.

Seismic signal processing and earthquake early warning

At BUET's Japan Institute of Disaster Prevention and Urban Safety, I curated records from 48 earthquakes collected by Bangladesh's national seismic network and studied the frequency content and attenuation of seismic waves in the Bengal Basin. I also developed a self-supervised contrastive graph neural network for predicting seismic intensity from early waveforms, published in IEEE Transactions on Geoscience and Remote Sensing in 2024.

Radar sensing and other applied machine learning

My research extends beyond wireless communications to mmWave radar ballistocardiography for contactless heart-rate estimation; GPS-denied geolocation through questionnaire-based inverse navigation (I-NAV); and embedded computer vision for automated railroad level crossings. I have also studied weather-related wireless latency on the NSF PAWR ARA testbed, presenting the work at MERIF 2024.
