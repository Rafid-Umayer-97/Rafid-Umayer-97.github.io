Rafid Umayer Murshed

PhD Candidate in Computer Science · University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign

I am a PhD candidate in the Siebel School of Computing and Data Science at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign (UIUC), where I work in the iSens Lab under Prof. Elahe Soltanaghai. My research lies at the intersection of wireless communications, machine learning, signal processing, and physical modeling.

My current work includes physics-informed neural operators for ray-tracing acceleration, measurement-driven wireless neural fields, compact channel representations for feedback and prediction, and GPU-accelerated wireless computation. My interests also extend to MU-MIMO and THz beamforming, metasurfaces, radar sensing, spatiotemporal learning, and real-world sensing systems. More broadly, I am interested in how learning algorithms can complement physical models and numerical simulation.

Before UIUC, I completed an M.Sc. in Electrical Engineering at The University of Texas at Dallas (2025), working with Prof. Mohammad Saquib on beamforming and metasurface design, and a B.Sc. in Electrical and Electronic Engineering at BUET (2022), advised by Dr. Md. Farhad Hossain. I also conducted earthquake early-warning research at BUET–JIDPUS. In 2026, I was a Machine Learning Research Intern at InterDigital AI Lab in Los Altos, California.

Research interests

I study physics-informed and scientific machine learning for wireless systems, including neural operators, radiance fields, numerical simulation, differentiable radio propagation, wireless channel modeling, 6G MIMO/THz beamforming, CSI feedback and tracking, intelligent surfaces, and radar sensing. I am also interested in graph learning for seismic early warning and data-driven modeling of complex physical processes.

At UIUC, I took CS 598: Scientific Machine Learning with Professors Luke Olson and Matthew West, studying machine learning for ordinary and partial differential equations, numerical integration, and physics-informed models.

Selected publications

A selection of peer-reviewed research spanning neural operators, neural fields, and real-world sensing. All illustrated figures are from the research manuscripts, not generated artwork.

Physics-Unrolled Neural Operator for Wireless Field Modeling

R. U. Murshed, S. U. Rahman, M. Tang, E. Soltanaghai

NeurIPS 2026 · Accepted

A multi-fidelity neural operator that progressively models reflection, diffraction, and scattering to predict high-fidelity wireless fields from inexpensive, noisy ray tracing. The paper establishes a conditional denoising result and reports approximately 250× faster inference than high-fidelity simulation.

PaperResearch

WiNeRF: Measurement Constrained Radiance Fields for Actionable Wireless Channel Modeling

S. U. Rahman, R. U. Murshed, A. Dmitriev, C. Tanriover, R. C. Shah, E. Soltanaghai

EWSN 2026 · Published

Learns continuous complex-valued wireless channel representations from sparse commodity Wi-Fi CSI while incorporating antenna geometry, phase ambiguities, and hardware measurement constraints.

PaperResearch

Real-time Seismic Intensity Prediction using Self-supervised Contrastive GNN for Earthquake Early Warning

R. U. Murshed et al.

IEEE Transactions on Geoscience and Remote Sensing · 2024

A self-supervised spatiotemporal graph neural network that uses short earthquake recordings from distributed seismic stations to predict shaking intensity for earthquake early warning.

GCNO: Gramian Chebyshev Neural Operator for Physics-Based Compression of Wireless Channels

R. U. Murshed, S. Hamidi-Rad, E. Soltanaghai, A. Malhotra

AAAI 2027 · Under review

An unsupervised, physics-based neural operator that compresses CSI into variable-length propagation-path descriptions, allowing analytical channel reconstruction and transfer to unseen antenna array sizes.

PreprintResearch

FoliaRT: A Reusable Neural Electromagnetic Canopy Model for Sub-6 GHz Wireless Ray Tracing

S. U. Rahman, R. U. Murshed, E. Soltanaghai

ACM SenSys 2027 · Under review

A reusable physics-informed model of directional radio scattering in vegetation, evaluated against electromagnetic simulation and measured data. The submitted paper reports up to 35,000× faster canopy-response prediction and a 53% reduction in median power-prediction error in its AERPAW case study.
